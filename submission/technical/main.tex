\DocSection{01 / OVERVIEW}{Technical Documentation}{2026 FinTechathon International Track\newline Topic E - SME Finance Copilot}

\DocNote{\textbf{Purpose.} Run the shop and use its records to make financial decisions. BizWizard combines business management with financing comparisons, cash-flow alerts and financial analysis. Code calculates the numbers; optional AI helps choose or explain allowed options.}

\subsection*{The business records behind the copilot}

Inventory, sales, purchases, customers, suppliers, debts, expenses and employee workflows share one platform. The finance tools use records saved through these workflows rather than asking owners to build a separate financial dataset. Demonstrations and submitted test evidence use synthetic data, not verified customer records.

\subsection*{Implemented capabilities}

\begin{ReportTable}{L{0.289421}L{0.710579}}

\rowcolor{Navy} \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Topic E capability}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Implemented workflow}} \\

Financing-option matching & Build a shop profile; apply eligibility rules; rank feasible products; price and negotiate simulated offers; create an owner-approved application and repayment schedule. \\

Cash-flow management & Maintain cash events and commitments; project a 90-day balance; raise rule-based alerts; propose guarded treasury actions and link recorded collection evidence. \\

Business financial analysis & Compute product economics and working-capital metrics; verify candidate explanations; compare scenarios; measure 30-day observations and matched sandbox outcomes. \\

\end{ReportTable}

\subsection*{Assessment criteria}

\textbf{Task completion (40\%):} the three workflows and their execution evidence. \textbf{Security \& compliance (30\%):} authorization, financial guards and risk assessment. \textbf{Innovation \& interaction (30\%):} structured AI choices, policy-based negotiation, editable mandates and reproducible paired evaluations.


\subsection*{Scope}

The finance copilot operates in a sandbox: financing offers and money movement are simulated. No external lender is contacted and no bank transfer is executed. The business-management platform supplies the records; this document focuses on the Topic E finance workflows and their limits.

\DocSection{03 / FINANCING}{Financing Algorithms}{Eligibility, affordability, offer ranking and bounded negotiation.}

\begin{ReportTable}{L{0.261477}L{0.738523}}

\rowcolor{Navy} \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Stage}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Rules enforced by code}} \\

1. Profile and completeness & Aggregate the shop history: revenue, COGS, operating expense, receivables, inventory, debt burden and cash. Matching requires at least 60\% coverage of its 90-day sales window. \\

2. Eligibility gate & Check amount, tenor, business age, revenue, debt/revenue, sector and required-document flags. NEAR\_MISS means exactly one soft threshold missed by at most 20\%; it is not automatic approval. \\

3. Rank feasible products & Weighted score: affordability 30\%, cost 25\%, liquidity 20\%, collateral 15\%, flexibility 10\%. AI enrichment is bounded to +/-15 score points; the blend is 60\% deterministic and 40\% bounded AI. \\

4. Price and negotiate & Initial offers pass product and affordability guards. Pricing and the accept/counter/reject policies are deterministic, with at most three rounds; they are not an LLM-to-LLM negotiation. \\

5. Owner confirmation & Acceptance checks shop access, offer state and expiry, then saves the application and rounded schedule in one database transaction. Repeated acceptance reuses it; affordability is not recalculated at this stage. \\

\end{ReportTable}

\subsection*{Money and affordability}

\begin{align*}
\mathrm{FCF} &= R - C - O - D, \\
\mathrm{EMI}_{\mathrm{screen}} &= 0.50\max(0,\mathrm{FCF}), \\
\frac{\mathrm{PMT}}{\mathrm{FCF}} &\leq 0.35 \quad (\mathrm{FCF}>0), \\
\mathrm{PMT} &=
\begin{cases}
P/n, & r=0, \\
Pr\big/\bigl(1-(1+r)^{-n}\bigr), & r>0,
\end{cases}
\qquad r=\frac{i_{\mathrm{annual}}}{1200}.
\end{align*}
\SmallText{FCF is free cash flow; COGS is cost of goods sold; EMI and PMT refer to monthly installments. R is monthly revenue, C is COGS, O is operating expense, D is existing debt service, P is principal, n is the number of installments, and the annual rate is a percentage.}

The 50\% screening capacity and 35\% borrower policy ceiling are separate checks. AI enriches product ranking; deterministic policies price and negotiate offers. Currency boundaries round to two decimals, with the last installment absorbing residual principal. Submission is not disbursement.


\DocNote[Amber]{\textbf{Model limitations.} Missing lot costs are estimated at 70\% of line sales value. Credit risk uses rules of thumb, not a validated bureau score. The effectiveApr helper annualizes total cost divided by principal; it is not a regulatory annual percentage rate (APR) based on the timing of payments. Quotes are simulated, not real credit commitments.}

\DocSection{04 / CASH FLOW AND ANALYSIS}{Cash Flow and Financial Analysis}{Cash synchronization, projection, alerts and financial analysis methods.}

\subsection*{Cash synchronization and projection}

PostgreSQL triggers keep cash records in step with non-simulated paid sales, debt payments and expenses, including edits and deletions. Demo shops use a separate simulator-managed ledger. Backfill adds missing historical cash entries without duplicating them or replacing manual entries. Settling a supplier obligation links an existing matching purchase; it does not issue another payment.


The projection builds daily historical inflow/outflow series, estimates recent levels and day-of-week seasonality, applies a clipped trend, and adds scheduled commitments and probability-weighted receivables. It rolls balances forward for 90 days and identifies the trough, first shortfall and buffer days.


\begin{ReportTable}{L{0.479042}L{0.520958}}

\rowcolor{Navy} \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Nine alert conditions}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Behavior}} \\

Shortfall within 14 days; buffer below target & Prioritize potential liquidity pressure; low-history rules suppress inappropriate confidence. \\

Commitment due soon; debt older than 30 / 60 days & Show dated obligations and aging receivables. The 30- and 60-day checks are separate conditions of the overdue alert family. \\

Idle cash; revenue decline; expense spike; insufficient data & Flag idle balances, a 7-day revenue decline relative to the prior average, expenses above twice their category median, or fewer than 14 ledger days. \\

\end{ReportTable}

\subsection*{Analysis and hypothesis verification}

The analysis module computes margins, product contribution, working capital, breakeven and concentration from the selected shop's records. It checks an explanation by subtracting its contribution from the observed gap. A claim is VERIFIED only when at most 10\% of the nonzero gap remains unexplained. This checks whether the numbers fit, not whether one event caused another.


Scenario levers change price, collections, operating cost or product mix through an explicit simplified model. Approved capital decisions freeze an observation window; 30-day before/after profit observations exclude future rows and remain non-causal. Treasury collection evidence links unique existing payments and is gross cash, not incremental profit.


\SmallText{\textbf{Assumptions:} collection probabilities and scenario elasticity are heuristics. Receivable age can stand in for overdue age. Scheduled commitments can overlap ordinary historical expenses. Cash accounts are not externally reconciled; historical supplier obligations must be recorded explicitly.}

\subsection*{Worked example: from a sale to evidence}

\DocNote{\textbf{Illustrative flow, not a measured result.} A paid sale of BDT 1,000 is saved in a non-demo shop. Its cash entry is synchronized automatically and feeds the next projection. If a cash-buffer alert appears, the owner can review a proposed collection plan before approving it. Approval alone adds no cash: recording a collection must link an existing valid payment. The saved plan and payment link let the reviewer inspect what was approved and what was actually recorded.}

\DocSection{05 / AI RUNTIME AND EVALUATION}{AI Runtime and Evaluation}{Structured model choices, deterministic validation and paired sandbox measurement.}

Each AI call requests JSON, checked with Zod against the expected fields and allowed values. One repair retry is permitted within the time limit. Missing keys, invalid output, exhausted budgets and timeouts use a clearly labelled rules-based fallback. Calls reserve part of the business's monthly AI budget. The system attempts to log the model, prompt version, tokens, estimated cost and status; a logging failure does not block the feature.


\subsection*{Shop-specific paired evaluation}

\EvaluationDiagram

The snapshot requires 60\% sales-history completeness and at least 14 cash-history days. A repeatable-read database transaction captures a consistent view of quotes, product economics, commitments and versioned owner policies (mandates). The AI cannot choose the random seed or future outcomes. A unique snapshot key and a single database claim prevent duplicate paid calls; unchanged daily inputs reuse the saved evaluation.


\begin{ReportTable}{L{0.221557}L{0.778443}}

\rowcolor{Navy} \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Role}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Paired sandbox metric}} \\

Lender & Net cash return over the loan term after funding costs, repayments and modeled recovery. AI and fixed-rate rules use the same loans, exposure limits and random default outcomes. \\

Borrower & Full-term borrowing cost on equal-principal feasible quotes; baseline selects lowest quoted APR. Both sides assume repayment in full, independently of the lender experiment. \\

Treasurer / analyst & 30-day closing cash versus delayed-collection rules; modeled capital gross margin versus equal allocation among products ranked by revenue. Each role is a separate experiment. \\

\end{ReportTable}

\DocNote{\textbf{Evaluation limitations.} Eight paired paths do not establish a real-world treatment effect. Quotes use captured current terms rather than reconstructed pre-negotiation contracts. Missing data stays pending, and losses and ties are retained. Sandbox results are reported separately from real-world outcomes.}

\DocSection{07 / DEPLOYMENT AND REPRODUCTION}{Deployment and Reproduction}{Prerequisites, execution commands and the reviewer workflow.}

\subsection*{Evaluation commands and prerequisites}

\begin{CodeBlock}
# From server/ after npm ci
npm run benchmark:finance
npm run benchmark:finance -- --live
npm run evidence:finance
npm run evidence:finance -- --replay EXPORTED_EVALUATION.json
\end{CodeBlock}

All commands need Node.js 24, npm, Git and installed dependencies. Offline benchmarks and JSON replay need no database or AI key. Only --live uses your key from server/.env.benchmark or the environment. Database evidence needs local PostgreSQL at 127.0.0.1, database simulator\_test and EVIDENCE\_DATABASE\_URL; it creates a fresh schema, applies migrations and disables external AI. See the README for setup steps.


\subsection*{Application deployment}

\textbf{Frontend:} Vercel, root client, Next.js preset; set NEXT\_PUBLIC\_API\_BASE\_URL to the backend /api/v1 endpoint. \textbf{Backend:} main-branch CI builds an artifact and deploys over restricted SSH. The installed script installs dependencies, generates Prisma, starts backup, applies migrations, switches releases and checks readiness. VPS secrets and services must already be configured; frontend deployment is separate.


Use migrate deploy, not schema-only db push: migrations install cash triggers, supplier-obligation fields and evaluation storage. Owners can backfill existing cash history. Code rollback does not undo database migrations.


\subsection*{Review workflow}

Use a synthetic account or demo workspace. Inspect a Cash Flow projection and alert; run financing matching and review negotiation and an approved application; inspect verified analysis. In AI Agent Ledger, run a matched evaluation, download it and replay the JSON. See the Security Self-Assessment for permissions and risks.
